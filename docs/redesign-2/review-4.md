# AstroSphere: independent design review (round 5 screenshots)

- **Reviewer:** independent design reviewer (Claude). I did not build the product, and I read no code, docs, earlier reviews or git history.
- **Date:** 2026-10-06.
- **Material:** the 15 PNGs in `docs/redesign-2/review-5/` and nothing else. Desktop shots are 1440×900 or 1280×800. Phone shots are 375×812 at 1 image px = 1 CSS px, and 14 is a 355×455 crop.
- **Method:** I viewed every screen at full size, then cropped and enlarged details with PIL. I applied the review questions of three disciplines:
  - UX: what is the task, and what is the next step?
  - Visual design: hierarchy, focal point, reading path.
  - Colour theory: functional colour, contrast, legibility.

**Three kinds of evidence**

- **Measured from pixels.** I sampled colours with PIL `getpixel` and used histograms to find the main background colour. Contrast is the WCAG 2.x ratio between that background and the 99.5th-percentile ink pixel. Cap and ascender heights count the rows whose luminance differs from the background by more than 40 % of the maximum. Ring radii and stroke widths come from radial scans of white pixels, and button sizes from edge scans. Panel sizes and area ratios come from the panel edges I read off the images, accurate to about ±5 px. I also scanned the scene canvases for accent-orange pixels (R>200, 80<G<140, B<80).
- **Judged by eye.** Attractiveness, hierarchy, reading path, collectible feel, wording.
- **Not judged.** Anything that needs motion or interaction:
  - the pulse animation and its calmness, the unlock animation, and reduced motion;
  - hover and tap tips, Usui-chan's explain mode, and keyboard focus order and Esc;
  - whether Enter or Space triggers "Hủy" in the dialog;
  - actual hit areas, which may be larger than the drawn shapes;
  - page `scrollWidth` on phones (I can only see that nothing visibly overflows);
  - the zones colouring. It is switched off in every screen, so I could not see which zone is drawn in which colour.

The fallback font in the screenshots renders Vietnamese diacritics imperfectly. I saw no spelling error that I could attribute to anything except that.

## Overall verdict

The product is visually coherent and attractive. The 3D dome is a strong focal point. Orange is used with discipline: only on interactive elements, none in the scene. The Simple first-run path is clear on both desktop and phone, and the USACodex states read without colour. What remains:

- one probable colour-meaning conflict (the status dot against the zones);
- discoverability in Full mode and on the phone header;
- layout pressure at 1280 with both panels open.

**It is ready to show the club now, and it is close to ready for a wider public launch, once the teal status-dot colour is checked against the zone colours (item 1) and Medium items 2–5 are fixed.**

## Coordinator note (2026-10-06): triage of the medium problems

- **#1 Status dot colour: false positive, caused by the reviewer brief.** The brief listed the zone colours in the wrong order. In `src/scene/colors.ts` and the `--zone-*` tokens, teal `#14b8a6` is **rise-set** (`riseSet`), purple is circumpolar and red is never-rises. The "Mọc và lặn" dot is therefore the correct zone colour. No change.
- **#2 Controls hidden in Full: owner decision.** The focus layout, with both drawers starting closed, was the owner's choice on 2026-10-05 ("Keep it"). It stays open for the owner to revisit; not changed in this round.
- **#3 Labels "below the horizon": not reproduced.** The labels over the ground disc belong to objects *above* the horizon. With the camera looking down on the dome, low southern stars project in front of the disc. At the LST of screen 05 (≈ 22h56m, φ = 21,03°), Phoenix stands at about +16° and Achernar at about +5°. Objects that are really below the horizon are hidden (`hideBelowHorizon`), or shown with the dashed ghost marker and label when selected.
- **#4 (crowding at 1280, both toggles open) and #5 (icon-only phone header)** were fixed in fix round 4, together with the quick low items #10, #11, #14 and #15; see `TODO.md` and the renders in `review-6/`.
